NICU vs Regular Nursery Care — What New Parents in Koppa Should Know

Published: July 20, 2026 Last Updated: July 27, 2026

Adarsh Hospital in Koppa, Chikkamagaluru, offers dedicated newborn services featuring both standard newborn nurseries for healthy, full-term infants and a Neonatal Intensive Care Unit (NICU) equipped with advanced thermoregulation, phototherapy, and continuous physiological monitoring for premature or high-risk babies.

Clinical Source: Led by co-founder Dr. Anitha N. Rao, MBBS MD DA DNB (KMC Reg. No. 35287) and senior obstetrician Dr. Nataraj R. Rao, MBBS MD DNB (KMC Reg. No. 31199).

The birth of a child is a monumental milestone, filled with anticipation and joy. Operating in Koppa since 2003, Adarsh Hospital has treated over 15,000 patients under its NABH-accredited since 2018 standards. While most deliveries result in healthy full-term infants who only require standard neonatal checkups, some newborns enter the world with unique medical needs that require specialized support. For expectant parents in Koppa and surrounding malnad areas, understanding the difference between the Neonatal Intensive Care Unit (NICU) and a regular newborn nursery helps build peace of mind during delivery.

What is a Regular Newborn Nursery?

A standard or regular newborn nursery is configured for healthy infants who are born near or at full term (37 to 42 weeks of gestation) and do not show signs of clinical distress. In a regular nursery setting:

  • The care focus is on routine clinical monitoring, including heart rate and breathing updates, weight checks, and basic temperature regulation.
  • Routine vaccinations (like BCG, Hepatitis B, and OPV) are administered.
  • Staff assist parents with initiating breastfeeding, basic swaddling, bathing, and baby hygiene care before discharge.
  • Infants spend most of their time in the same room as the mother (rooming-in) to promote early bonding.

What is a Neonatal Intensive Care Unit (NICU)?

The NICU is a specialized critical care ward designed specifically for premature babies (born before 37 weeks), low-birth-weight infants, or newborns with complex medical conditions (such as respiratory distress, neonatal jaundice, infections, or congenital issues). In the NICU environment:

  • Continuous Physiological Monitoring: Infants are connected to specialized systems that track oxygen levels, respiratory rates, heart activity, and blood pressure 24/7.
  • Advanced Thermoregulation: Premature babies lack the body fat needed to maintain their temperature. The NICU uses closed incubators or open warming pods to keep them in a stable thermal environment.
  • Specialized Respiratory and Nutritional Support: Babies may receive respiratory assistance (such as CPAP or mechanical ventilation) or receive specialized intravenous nutrition (TPN) or feeding tubes if their sucking reflexes are not yet developed.
  • Phototherapy: Blue light systems are used to safely break down bilirubin levels in infants showing signs of moderate-to-severe neonatal jaundice.

NICU Availability at Adarsh Hospital Koppa

Having local access to neonatal intensive care is incredibly vital. In emergency situations where a newborn requires breathing support, waiting hours to travel to a larger city can lead to critical complications.

Adarsh Hospital's dedicated Paediatric & Neonatal Department in Koppa is equipped with neonatal warming pods, phototherapy units, and critical monitoring systems. This allows our resident paediatricians and neonatal nurses to manage high-risk deliveries and provide immediate stabilization, and care for premature and vulnerable infants close to home.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Adarsh Hospital in Koppa have a NICU?

Yes, Adarsh Hospital has a dedicated Neonatal Intensive Care Unit (NICU) to provide critical support for premature, low-birth-weight, or ill newborns locally in Koppa.

What is the difference between NICU and nursery care?

NICU provides 24/7 continuous monitoring, respiratory help, and incubator warming for vulnerable babies, while regular nurseries provide basic monitoring and routine vaccine shots for healthy full-term infants.
